## 0. My own derivation: the hinge graph `Gamma`, and the closed form of deployment

Everything in this file rests on one object the 2026 paper never introduces.

**Definition (hinge graph).** `Gamma = (F, E_hinge)`: one node per face of `M`, one edge per
hinge-cut edge, joining the two faces incident to it. (`notes/field_rigidity.md` Sec. 0 introduces
the same graph for a *rigidity* purpose — body-and-pin counting. I use it for a completely
different purpose: as the domain of a discrete potential problem. The counting there fails, per
F13; the potential problem here does not.)

**(0.1) `Gamma` is planar and bipartite. [proved]**
`Gamma` is the dual of `M` with the outer-face node deleted and the split-cut dual edges deleted,
so it is a subgraph of a planar graph, hence planar. By F1, a hinge edge joins faces of *opposite*
`sigma`, so `sigma : F -> {-1,+1}` is a proper 2-colouring of `Gamma`. Bipartite.

**(0.2) Uniform deployment forces `omega_f = sigma(f) * theta/2`. [proved]**
Faces are rigid, so face `f` moves by `T_f^theta(x) = R(omega_f) x + t_f`, `T_f^0 = id`.
The opening angle of a hinge edge between `f_1, f_2` is the angle between the two duplicates,
i.e. `|omega_{f_1} - omega_{f_2}|`. Uniform deployability (2026 Sec. 3) says this equals `theta`
for **every** hinge edge, and all hinges open the same way, which fixes the sign:
`omega_{f_1} - omega_{f_2} = sigma(f_1) * theta`. On the connected bipartite `Gamma` the unique
solution up to an additive constant (a global rotation) is

```
        omega_f  =  sigma(f) * theta / 2 .                                        (0.2)
```

This answers `notes/paper_2026.md` open question 19 verbatim ("the sign pattern of face rotations
in the presence of split cuts, where the two-colouring that would fix the signs does not exist") —
the two-colouring *does* exist, not on `M` but on `Gamma`, and it is `sigma` itself.

**Immediate corollary: Remark A.4 in one line. [proved]** Across a split edge `sigma(f_1) = sigma(f_2)`
(F1), so by (0.2) the two faces rotate by the *same* angle, so the two duplicates of that edge are
related by a pure translation and stay parallel at every `theta`. Appendix A of the 2026 paper spends
three remarks, Eqs. (15)–(24) and an induction on the split forest to prove this.

**(0.3) The translation field is a discrete potential on `Gamma`. [proved]**
The hinge cut preserves the *source* vertex `u_e` (F1), so the hinge constraint is
`T_{f_1}^theta(x_{u_e}) = T_{f_2}^theta(x_{u_e})`. With `R(a) = cos(a) I + sin(a) J`,
`J = [[0,-1],[1,0]]`, and `R(a) - R(-a) = 2 sin(a) J`:

```
        t_{f_2} - t_{f_1}  =  2 sigma(f_1) sin(theta/2) * J x_{u_e} .              (0.3)
```

So `t` is a potential on `Gamma` with prescribed edge increments. It exists iff the increments sum
to zero around every cycle of `Gamma`. Since `2 sin(theta/2) J` is invertible for `theta != 0`, the
condition is **`theta`-independent**:

```
        sum_{e in gamma}  eps_e * sigma(tail-face(e)) * x_{u_e}  =  0 ,   for every cycle gamma of Gamma.   (0.4)
```

**(0.5) (0.4) *is* Eq. (2), and holes are the faces of `Gamma`. [proved for the face-cycle basis]**
The cycle space of a connected planar `Gamma` is generated by its bounded face boundaries. Take a
bounded face of `Gamma` around an interior vertex `v` of `M` with no split edges at `v`: by
Remark A.1 the hinge edges at `v` alternate in/out and `sigma` alternates around `v`, so the cycle
sum in (0.4) telescopes to `k_v x_v - sum_{u -> v} x_u`, which is exactly Eq. (2) for the hole
preimage `C_v = {in-edges of v}` (F11). **Hand-checked in full for the degree-4 case** (four faces
`f_1..f_4`, `sigma = +,-,+,-`, hinge edges alternating out/in/out/in with tails `v, u_2, v, u_4`):
(0.4) gives `x_v - x_{u_2} + x_v - x_{u_4} = 0`, i.e. `2 x_v = x_{u_2} + x_{u_4}`, which is Eq. (2).
Deleting a split-cut dual edge from `Gamma` *merges* two of its bounded faces, which is exactly
"split cuts merge holes" (2026 Sec. 4.2). So:

```
        holes of M'   <->   bounded faces of Gamma  (bijection).                   (0.5)
```

This is a **proof of F11** by planar duality, not the flood-fill argument of Alg. 1.

**(0.6) `H = V_int - |E_split|`, exactly. [proved]**
Euler on `Gamma` (connected, planar, `|F|` nodes, `|E_hinge|` edges): bounded faces
`= |E_hinge| - |F| + 1`. Euler on a disk-topology `M`: `N - E + |F| = 1` with
`E = E_border + E_hinge + E_split`, and `N - E_border = V_int` for a disk. Substituting,

```
        H  =  |E_hinge| - |F| + c(Gamma)  =  V_int - |E_split| + (c(Gamma) - 1) .        (0.6)
```

**I got this wrong on the first pass and the numerics caught it.** My first version dropped
`c(Gamma) - 1`, matching `notes/field_tutte.md` (2.1). It is correct only when `Gamma` is connected.
**Verified numerically** (`scratchpad/check3.cpp`, 400 random-`sigma` trials on a 6x6 patch): the
uncorrected form fails 400/400, the corrected form fails 0/400. With random `sigma`, `c(Gamma)`
reached 10 and `V_int - |E_split|` went as low as `-7` while `H = 2`. Under the paper's own
connectivity principle (Sec. 4.2 (1)) `c(Gamma) = 1` and (0.6) reduces to `H = V_int - |E_split|`,
which **proves** 2026 Sec. 4.4 claim 3 (`H <= V_int`, equality iff `E_split = {}`) as an identity.
My route needs neither F11 nor Remark A.4 as hypotheses — it derives them — and it says exactly what
goes wrong without connectivity, which field_tutte (2.1) does not.

Torus check (`chi = 0`, `c = 1`): faces of `Gamma` `= |E_hinge| - |F|`, `|F| = E - N`, giving
`H = N - |E_split| = V_int - |E_split|`. Consistent.

**Second corollary [proved]:** split-cut edges form a cycle in `M` **iff** the corresponding dual
edges form a cut of `Gamma`, i.e. iff `Gamma` is disconnected, i.e. iff `M'` is disconnected. So
Remark A.4's unproved acyclicity assumption (2026 assumption 6) is *equivalent* to the connectivity
principle of Sec. 4.2, not merely implied by it.

**(0.7) THE CLOSED FORM. [proved]**
Solve (0.3): with `t_{f_0} = 0`, `t_f = 2 sin(theta/2) J w_f` where `w_f = sum along any path in
Gamma of sigma(.) x_{u_.}` is a fixed vector, **linear in `X` and independent of `theta`**. Then the
deployed position of the copy of vertex `u` belonging to face `f` is

```
   y_{(u,f)}(theta) = R(sigma_f theta/2) x_u + t_f
                    = cos(theta/2) x_u + sin(theta/2) J (sigma_f x_u + 2 w_f) ,
```
using `cos(sigma a) = cos a`, `sin(sigma a) = sigma sin a`. Writing `chi_{(u,f)} := sigma_f x_u + 2 w_f`,

```
        Y_theta  =  cos(theta/2) * X'  +  sin(theta/2) * J (C X) .                 (0.7)
```

`X'` is the duplication map (each copy inherits its original position) and `C` is a fixed real matrix
built from `Gamma`, `sigma` and a spanning tree of `Gamma` alone. **Deployment is an explicit
trigonometric curve, linear in the design variables `X`.** The 2026 paper (Sec. 4.5, Sec. 8.3 of the
notes) treats forward kinematics `g` as an unwritten black box; open question 19 asks for it.

**(0.8) Consequences of (0.7) I use below. [proved unless marked]**
- **Period vectors.** For any identified pair, `p(theta) = cos(theta/2) p_0 + sin(theta/2) q`,
  `q` fixed. Hence `P_theta = cos(theta/2) P_0 + sin(theta/2) Q` and
  `J(theta) = P_theta P_0^{-1} = cos(theta/2) I + sin(theta/2) W`, `W := Q P_0^{-1}` **constant**.
- **Any two deployed edge vectors** are `d_i(theta) = cos(theta/2) a_i + sin(theta/2) J b_i`. Their
  cross product, using `c^2 = (1+cos theta)/2`, `s^2 = (1-cos theta)/2`, `cs = sin(theta)/2` and
  `Jb_1 x Jb_2 = b_1 x b_2`:
```
   d_1 x d_2 = (1/2)(a_1 x a_2 + b_1 x b_2)
             + (cos theta / 2)(a_1 x a_2 - b_1 x b_2)
             + (sin theta / 2)(a_1 x J b_2 + J b_1 x a_2)
             =  P + Q cos theta + R sin theta .                                    (0.8)
```
  **Every collinearity event is a root of `P + Q cos theta + R sin theta = 0`**, i.e. at most two
  roots in `[0, 2pi)`, in closed form, with `P, Q, R` *quadratic* in `X`.
- **Vertex-Laplacian factorization.** Row `K` of `L` is `sum_{v in K} ( k_v x_v - sum_{u->v} x_u )`,
  so `L = A_S * Delta_h`, where `Delta_h = diag(indeg) - Adj_in` is the `N x N` asymmetric hinge
  Laplacian and `A_S` is the `H x N` split-component/vertex incidence. Different factorization from
  `notes/field_tutte.md` (0.1) `L = R D` (edge-level); this one is vertex-level and gives
  `rank(L) <= min(H, rank Delta_h)` and hence `dim(shape space) >= 2 max(N - H, dim ker Delta_h)`.

### G1. Closed-form uniform deployment on arbitrary planar graphs
**Type: theorem (+ closed-form characterization).**

**Claim.** For any uniformly deployable kirigami `M'` on an arbitrary planar graph with any
`sigma`, every face rotates by exactly `omega_f = sigma(f) theta / 2` and
`Y_theta = cos(theta/2) X' + sin(theta/2) J (C X)`, where `X'` and `C` are fixed matrices determined
by `Gamma`, `sigma` and a spanning tree of `Gamma`, both linear in `X` and independent of `theta`;
consequently the entire deployment path is a single trigonometric curve, linear in the design
variables.

**Gap targeted.** 2026 Sec. 4.5, p. 60:9: "`g` is built analytically using forward kinematics" —
never written down. `notes/paper_2026.md` open question 19: "The root-face and propagation
convention of the forward-kinematics map `g`, **including the sign pattern of face rotations in the
presence of split cuts, where the two-colouring that would fix the signs does not exist**". Also
2026 Sec. 8.3's loose "each face undergoes a rigid rotation by `theta`".

**Novelty vs field.** R15 (Grima–Evans) has the closed form for *rotating squares only*, one tile
shape, and asserts rather than proves 1-DOF (field_rigidity R15). R7/R18 (Konakovic; Dudte–Choi)
have it for the fixed triangular and quad linkages. **R22 IsoGami** computes deployment by
*numerical* compliant-mode continuation with IPC contact and adaptive bisection at turning points
(isogami.txt Secs. 3.2, Eq. (9)); a group that had this closed form would not have written that
algorithm. field_tutte row 13 (parallel redrawing) notes the morph is parallel but gives no formula.

**Why it might be true / sketch.** Sec. 0 (0.2), (0.3), (0.7) above; all steps hand-checked. The one
place to be careful is the sign convention `omega_{f_1} - omega_{f_2} = sigma(f_1) theta`: this
encodes "all hinges open, none close", and its consistency is exactly bipartiteness of `Gamma`
(0.1), which holds because `sigma` 2-colours `Gamma` by construction (F1).

**Kill experiment.** *Already run for the pure case* (see (0.9)): on a 5x5 checkerboard patch the
closed form matches rigid-motion forward kinematics to `2e-15`, all opening angles equal `theta` to
12 decimals, and faces stay rigid to `1e-15`. Full version: build `Gamma`, compute `sigma_f theta/2`
and `w_f` by BFS on a spanning tree of `Gamma`; independently run the reference forward-kinematics routine
`core/forward_kinematics.cpp` at 50 values of `theta in (0, theta_max)` on **200 graphs**
(100 random Voronoi/Delaunay-dual, 100 hand-authored incl. non-2-colourable and split-heavy) and
compare vertex positions. **If `max_i ||y_i^FK - y_i^closedform|| > 1e-9` on any graph, dead.**
Runtime: seconds.

**If it survives, the demo.** Deployment at any `theta` with one matrix multiply; a table of
FK-vs-closed-form residuals over 200 graphs; and G2/G3 below, which are its corollaries.

**Risk.** That it is judged "obvious once stated". Mitigation: it must be sold bundled with G2 and
G3, which are the results it buys and which the paper explicitly leaves open.

**Effort.** Derivation S (done). Code S.

### G2. Infinitesimal conformality implies exact conformality: `J(theta) = cos(theta/2) I + sin(theta/2) W`
**Type: theorem.**

**Claim.** For a periodic uniformly deployable pattern, `J(theta) = cos(theta/2) I + sin(theta/2) W`
with `W = Q P_0^{-1}` a constant matrix; hence `J(theta)` is a similarity for **all** `theta` if and
only if `W` is a similarity, if and only if the two linear constraints of Eq. (13)
(`d(J_11 - J_22)/dtheta = 0`, `d(J_12 + J_21)/dtheta = 0` at `theta = 0`) hold. The deployment scale
is `s(theta) = |cos(theta/2) + lambda sin(theta/2) e^{i psi}|` where `W = lambda R(psi)`.

**Gap targeted.** 2026 Sec. 5.1, p. 60:12, verbatim and load-bearing:
"**Moreover, once the derivative of the conformal distortion vanishes at `theta = 0`, the deployment
remains conformal for all opening angles `theta`.**" `notes/paper_2026.md` Sec. 13.2 lists this as a
heuristic supported only by "Empirically, we observe..." with no proof and no experiment count;
assumption 19; open question 15.

**Novelty vs field.** R7 Konakovic-Lukovic et al. 2018 and R5 2016 *use* known isotropic linkages
and never state a Jacobian identity. R22 IsoGami measures deployment behaviour numerically per
design and has no conformality theorem. R17 Borcea–Streinu "geometric auxetics" (field_rigidity R17)
studies periodic auxetic *cones* of deformations, not the metatile Jacobian of a hinged tiling under
a single uniform angle, and the orchestrator already checked their 2020 paper is about one specific
3-DOF framework (STATE.md).

**Why it might be true / sketch.** From (0.8): every period vector is `cos(theta/2) p_0 +
sin(theta/2) q` because it is a difference of two instances of (0.7). So `P_theta P_0^{-1} =
cos(theta/2) I + sin(theta/2) W`. The similarity conditions `J_11 = J_22`, `J_12 = -J_21` are linear
in the entries of `J`, and `cos(theta/2) I` satisfies them identically, so they hold for all `theta`
iff they hold for `W`. And `dJ/dtheta|_0 = (1/2) W` because the `cos(theta/2)` term has zero
derivative at `0`. Therefore Eq. (13)'s infinitesimal constraint is *identical* to the global one.
Note the sharper consequence: **Eq. (13) is not an approximation at all**, which is stronger than
what the authors claim empirically.

**Kill experiment.** On **30 periodic patterns** (the paper's Figs. 21/22-style tilings plus random
periodic Voronoi metatiles), solve Eq. (13) with `core/conformal_solve.cpp`, then sample `theta` on a
200-point grid over `(0, theta_max)`, assemble `P_theta` from the FK output, and measure
`max_theta ( |J_11 - J_22| + |J_12 + J_21| ) / ||J||`. **If it exceeds `1e-9` on any pattern, the
theorem is dead** (and the paper's empirical claim is dead with it, which is also publishable).
Second check: fit `P_theta` to `cos(theta/2) P_0 + sin(theta/2) Q` by least squares and require
residual `< 1e-10`. Runtime: minutes.

**If it survives, the demo.** A proof; a plot of conformal distortion vs `theta` (machine zero) for
30 patterns against a non-conformal control; the closed-form scale curve `s(theta)` overlaid on
measured metatile areas; and the exact Poisson-ratio statement `nu = -1` for all `theta`, derived
rather than observed.

**Risk.** That some periodic pattern has `P_theta` not expressible as a difference of two (0.7)
instances — e.g. if the periodic identification pairs copies in *different* faces whose `w_f` differ
by a `theta`-dependent amount. It cannot: `w_f` is `theta`-independent by construction. The real
risk is that a referee calls it a one-line corollary of G1. It is; that is the point.

**Effort.** Derivation S (done). Code S.

### G3. Exact collision times: every collinearity event solves `P + Q cos(theta) + R sin(theta) = 0`
**Type: characterization (+ algorithm).**

**Claim.** For any ordered pair of edges of `M'`, the set of deployment angles at which they are
collinear is exactly the root set of `P + Q cos(theta) + R sin(theta) = 0` with `P, Q, R` explicit
quadratic forms in `X`; hence `theta_max` is the minimum over `O(|E'|)` pairs of a closed-form
arctangent, computable exactly with no bisection and differentiable in `X` almost everywhere.

**Gap targeted.** 2026 Sec. 6 limitation 1, verbatim: "such issues can only be detected through
explicit deployment simulation via forward kinematics and require additional post-processing to
resolve. **Developing geometric characteristics for favorable deployment behavior directly from the
embedding remains an open problem.**" Also Sec. 4.5's admitted-empirical "collisions occur only
along split-cut edges" (assumption 17) and Sec. 4.5's `theta = 0` surrogate.

**Novelty vs field.** field_kirigami seed 1 verdict: **open**, "the base paper names it as an open
problem verbatim". R18 Liu et al. 2024 gives a closed-form inequality but only for *enumerated pair
types* of quad units with curved contours and falls back to a manual UI otherwise. R22 IsoGami uses
IPC barriers and step bisection — a numerical, not closed-form, treatment, and only for isohedral
tilings. R1/R15 rotating squares have `theta_max = 2pi - alpha` for one shape.

**Why it might be true / sketch.** (0.8), fully derived above. The key cancellation is
`J b_1 x J b_2 = b_1 x b_2` (rotations preserve the cross product), which collapses what looks like a
degree-2 trigonometric polynomial in `theta/2` into a degree-1 one in `theta`. Roots:
`theta = atan2(R, Q) +/- arccos(-P / sqrt(Q^2 + R^2))` when `|P| <= sqrt(Q^2+R^2)`, no root
otherwise. Collinearity is *necessary* for contact (2026 Sec. 4.5 "collisions arise immediately
after some pair(s) of edges becomes collinear"), so screening the roots by an overlap test gives
`theta_max` exactly.

**Kill experiment.** On **300 graphs** (200 random, 100 hand-authored) compute `theta_max` two ways:
(a) closed form from the root formula plus an overlap screen; (b) the reference bisection routine
`core/theta_max_bisect.cpp` at tolerance `1e-8`. **If `|theta_max^(a) - theta_max^(b)| > 1e-6` on any
graph, dead.** A secondary, separately interesting output: on the same run, record whether the
binding pair is a split-edge pair. **If a non-split pair ever binds first, 2026 assumption 17 is
falsified** — which is a result either way. Runtime: under 10 minutes.

**If it survives, the demo.** `theta_max` speed and accuracy vs bisection on 300 graphs; the
counterexample gallery (if any) to "collisions only at split edges"; and it is the objective inside
G10.

**Risk.** Collinearity may be necessary but not sufficient, so the overlap screen (a combinatorial
step) could be the actual difficulty and could reintroduce simulation. Mitigation: the screen only
has to be evaluated *at the candidate roots*, a finite set, not swept over `theta`.

**Effort.** Derivation S (done). Code M.

### G4. Holes are the faces of the hinge graph; `H = V_int - |E_split|` by Euler
**Type: theorem.**

**Claim.** The holes of `M'` are in bijection with the bounded faces of `Gamma = (F, E_hinge)`;
consequently `H = |E_hinge| - |F| + c(Gamma) = V_int - |E_split| + (c(Gamma) - 1)` for disk
topology, so `H = V_int - |E_split|` exactly when `M'` is connected, with `H = V_int` iff
`E_split = {}`. Moreover `E_split` contains a cycle of `M` iff `Gamma` is disconnected iff `M'` is
disconnected.

**Gap targeted.** 2026 Sec. 4.4 claim 3: "`#holes <= #interior vertices`, since adjacent split cuts
merge multiple edges into a single hole" — asserted, no proof (`notes/paper_2026.md` Sec. 7.3,
assumptions 12, 13; open questions 1, 5, 9). Also Remark A.4's acyclicity, which the paper concedes
"is *not a theorem*; it is a consequence of an assumption" (notes Sec. 5, Remark A.4, step 3).

**Novelty vs field.** field_tutte (2.1) gets the connected-case identity but **conditionally on F11
and on Remark A.4** and via a split-forest count, and it is *false* without connectivity (400/400
random-`sigma` counterexamples, above); my route derives F11 and A.4 rather than assuming them, and
gives the correct general form. field_tutte row 11 (Whiteley hypergraph matroid) and row 12
(Servatius–Whiteley) are generic matroid theories and give only bounds (route A3, "discard").
R17 Dudte et al. 2023's additive framework counts four-bar linkages in quad tilings only.

**Why it might be true / sketch.** (0.5) and (0.6) above. The bijection is planar duality: `Gamma` is
the interior dual with split-dual edges deleted, and deleting a dual edge merges two dual faces —
which is precisely the paper's own sentence "split-cut edges merge otherwise local holes into larger
ones" (Sec. 4.2), now a theorem rather than an observation. The Euler computation is two lines and
is verified above in both the disk (`chi = 1`) and torus (`chi = 0`) cases.

**Kill experiment.** *Already run in reduced form* on a 6x6 square patch with 400 random `sigma`
(`scratchpad/check3.cpp`): 0/400 failures of the corrected identity, plus 0 failures of Remark A.1.
Full version: on **500 random planar graphs** (Voronoi, Delaunay, random-quad-subdivision;
100–5,000 faces) with **random `sigma`** (not the max-cut `sigma` — random `sigma` stresses the
split-heavy regime), run `core/hole_preimage.cpp` (Algorithm 1 with the three prose rules) to get
`H`, and independently build `Gamma` and count its bounded faces via `|E_hinge| - |F| + c(Gamma)`.
**If the two counts differ on any graph, dead.** Also assert every hinge edge lies in exactly one
preimage (F11). Runtime: minutes.

**If it survives, the demo.** A histogram of `H` vs `V_int - |E_split|` over 500 graphs sitting
exactly on the diagonal, plus the disconnection experiment: deliberately construct a `sigma` with a
split cycle and show `Gamma` splits and `M'` falls apart, closing 2026 open question 24.

**Risk.** F11 is false — a hinge edge really does bound two different holes — in which case `H` in
Eq. (4) is not the row count and the whole bijection needs restating with multiplicity. That is
exactly what the kill experiment tests first, and a *negative* result is a finding about the paper.

**Effort.** Derivation S (done). Code S.

### G5. The corank of `L` is the number of closed classes of the hole digraph
**Type: theorem (+ counterexample search).**

**Claim.** `rank(L) = H - dim Z` where `Z = ker(S - W)`, `S, W` the `N x H` row-stochastic matrices
`S[v,K] = [K(v) = K]`, `W[v,K] = (1/k_v) #{hinge edges v->w : K(w) = K}`; the maximum principle gives
`dim Z <= #` closed communicating classes of the hole digraph `Lambda` (`K -> K'` iff some `v in K`
has a hinge out-edge into `K'`), with equality iff the absorption-probability system is consistent.
In particular **`corank(L) >= 2` is achievable**, so the shape space can exceed
`2(|E_split| + 1)`.

**Gap targeted.** 2026 Sec. 4.4 claim 2: "the number of independent equations ... equals the number
of holes" — never argued (`notes/paper_2026.md` Sec. 7.3, assumption 14, open question 7).

**Novelty vs field.** field_tutte route A1 proves `dim Z = 1` for the strongly connected periodic
case and A2 gives `dim Z = 0` under an unstated boundary-reachability hypothesis; it explicitly does
not treat the intermediate case, and its summary table marks A2 "known technique". The claim
`corank >= 2` **contradicts** the natural reading of both. field_tutte row 7 (Colin de Verdiere
null space) is the only literature giving combinatorial meaning to a null space of a planar graph,
and it needs a symmetric matrix with the Strong Arnold Property; `L` is not symmetric.

**Why it might be true / sketch.** Max principle: let `y` attain its max at hole `K*`; for any
`v in K*`, `y_{K*}` is a mean of `y` over the out-holes of `v`, all `<= max`, so all equal `max`.
So argmax is closed under `Lambda`-out-neighbours. Hence `dim Z <=` #closed classes. Conversely
`1_T` for a closed class `T` fails at transient vertices (`S y = 0` but `W y > 0`), so equality needs
the harmonic extension to solve the *over-determined* per-vertex system (`|K|` equations per hole,
not one) — that is the real content and the plausible failure point. **Construction for
`corank >= 2`:** two split components each of whose vertices have all hinge out-edges staying inside
the component (two "sink islands"), joined only by hinge edges pointing inward. Whether such a
`sigma` exists on a 3-connected planar graph is exactly what the counterexample search decides.

**Kill experiment.** Two parts, both in `core/`. (a) On **500 random graphs with random `sigma`**,
compute `rank(L)` by rank-revealing QR (`Eigen::ColPivHouseholderQR`, tol `1e-10 * ||L||`) and
compute #closed classes of `Lambda` by Tarjan SCC + terminality test. **If `rank(L) != H - #closed
classes` on any graph, the equality half is dead** (the inequality half survives and is still a
theorem). (b) Exhaustive/greedy search over `sigma` on ~2,000 small graphs (10–40 faces) for
`corank(L) >= 2`. **If no instance with `corank >= 2` is found in 2,000 graphs x 200 `sigma` each,
that half of the claim is dead**, and the paper's claim 2 stands modulo the corank-1 periodic
correction. Runtime: under 20 minutes.

**If it survives, the demo.** The smallest `corank = 2` graph as a hero figure, with its two
independent extra shape-space directions rendered; a scatter of `corank(L)` vs #closed classes over
500 graphs; a corrected statement of Eq. (4)'s row count.

**Risk.** `dim Z` is generically `1` (periodic) or `0` (fixed boundary) and the intermediate case is
vacuous on 3-connected planar graphs, making the theorem true but empty. That would still refute the
paper's claim 2 in the periodic case, which is the minimum deliverable.

**Effort.** Derivation M. Code M.

### G6. `E_split` is a T-join: exact face orientations in polynomial time, and a lower bound on the shape space
**Type: theorem + algorithm.**

**Claim.** At every interior vertex `deg_split(v) = deg(v) mod 2`, so `E_split` is a T-join for
`T = {interior v : deg(v) odd}` (boundary vertices free); hence
`|E_split| >= min-T-join >= ceil(|T|/2)` for **every** `sigma`, and the `sigma` minimizing
`|E_split|` is computable **exactly in polynomial time** by Edmonds matching on the `T`-metric
closure — max-cut on the planar dual `M_d` is not NP-hard. Combining with G4:
`dim(shape space) >= 2 (min-T-join + dim Z) >= |T| + 2 dim Z`.

**Gap targeted.** 2026 Sec. 4.2, p. 60:6: "**Since max-cut is NP-hard**, we use the following
approximation, based on the algorithm proposed by Goemans and Williamson [1995]" — Eq. (1).
`notes/paper_2026.md` Sec. 6.5 records that this is not the GW SDP at all (unit circle in `R^2`, a
non-convex problem with **no approximation guarantee**), that the diameter rounding rule is
unspecified, and that the only support offered is "For all tested patterns, this algorithm produces
well-formed kirigami structures".

**Novelty vs field.** field_kirigami seed 4 verdict "partly open", with **R22 IsoGami** as the
threat — but IsoGami enumerates `3^m` joint configurations with heuristic hierarchical filtering
(isogami.txt Sec. 4.1) and is restricted to isohedral unit cells of `<= 16` edges; it has no
polynomial exact algorithm and no lower bound. R9 Chen–Choi–Mahadevan 2020 varies topology by
stochastic search at fixed geometry. Nothing in field_tutte or field_kirigami connects `sigma` to
T-joins or to planar max-cut.

**Why it might be true / sketch.** Remark A.1 gives `deg_hinge(v)` even at interior `v` (I re-derive
it in one line from (0.1): `E_hinge` is a cut of `Gamma`, hence its primal image is an even subgraph
modulo boundary edges). So `deg_split(v) = deg(v) - deg_hinge(v) = deg(v) mod 2`. A subgraph with
prescribed odd-degree set `T` is a T-join; minimum T-join is Edmonds-solvable in `O(n^3)`. The
converse (every T-join is realizable by some `sigma`) needs: `E_int \ E_split` plus a chosen subset
of boundary edges is an even subgraph of `M`, hence a cut of the full planar dual, hence a `sigma`.
Boundary parity is fixable because the boundary is a single cycle and the number of odd boundary
vertices is even. **[sketch — the boundary-fixing step is the one I did not fully close.]**
The bound `min-T-join >= ceil(|T|/2)` is immediate (one edge fixes at most two odd vertices).

**Kill experiment.** The parity half is *already verified* on 400 random-`sigma` trials x 25 interior
vertices with zero failures ((0.9)) — but only on a square grid, where every interior vertex has even
degree, so the interesting case `|T| > 0` is untested and must be run on odd-degree graphs.
Full version: on **500 random graphs** (mixture of odd- and even-degree-heavy), (a) assert
`deg_split(v) = deg(v) mod 2` at every interior vertex for `sigma` produced by the reference Eq. (1)
solver and for 50 random `sigma` each — **any violation kills the parity theorem outright**;
(b) compute min-T-join `|E_split|*` with a blossom implementation and compare against the reference
Eq. (1) + diameter rounding. **If Eq. (1) ever produces `|E_split| < |E_split|*`, the T-join
formulation is wrong.** The headline number is the mean gap `(|E_split|_GW - |E_split|*) /
|E_split|*` over 500 graphs; a gap below 2% would make the algorithmic half uninteresting (though
the theorem half survives). Runtime: minutes for (a), under 20 for (b) at these sizes.

**If it survives, the demo.** Exact-vs-heuristic `|E_split|` on `>= 20` graphs including all the
paper's named tilings; a plot of shape-space dimension vs `|T|` confirming the lower bound; and the
punchline figure: a non-2-colourable tiling where the exact `sigma` gives strictly fewer, more
evenly distributed split cuts than Eq. (1).

**Risk.** That the GW-style heuristic is already optimal or near-optimal on all realistic tilings,
leaving only a theoretical improvement. Also that the paper's true objective is not `|E_split|` but
the unquantified "many balanced, small holes" (Sec. 4.2, flagged in notes Sec. 13.2 as supported by
"Nothing"), in which case exact minimization optimizes the wrong thing — which is itself worth
saying, and G6's lower bound makes the trade-off explicit: fewer split edges means a *smaller*
design space (notes Sec. 13.2 records the paper never discusses this tension).

**Effort.** Derivation M. Code M (needs a blossom implementation; a simple `O(n^3)` one suffices at
these sizes).

### G7. Tutte auxetic embeddings are split-component-averaged harmonic maps
**Type: characterization.**

**Claim.** `L = A_S * Delta_h` where `Delta_h = diag(indeg_h) - Adj_in` is the asymmetric hinge
Laplacian and `A_S` is the split-component incidence; so `X` is a Tutte auxetic embedding iff the
harmonic residual `rho(v) := (Delta_h X)(v) in R^2` sums to zero on every split component. Hence
`null(L) = Delta_h^{-1}(B)` with `B = {rho : A_S rho = 0}`, and
`dim(shape space) >= 2 max(N - H, dim ker Delta_h)`.

**Gap targeted.** 2026 Sec. 4.4: "`L` [is] constructed analogously to the graph Laplacian" — an
analogy, never made precise; and Eq. (5)'s interpretation "each `phi_i` identifies a set of vertices
that can be translated together", which is a description of a computed SVD basis, not a
characterization (`notes/paper_2026.md` Sec. 7.4, open questions 7, 10, 11).

**Novelty vs field.** field_tutte (0.1) factors `L = R D` at the **edge** level and (0.5) notes the
pure case is a partial-star convex combination; it does **not** give the vertex-level factorization,
the residual field `rho`, or the resulting dimension bound. field_tutte rows 1–3 (Tutte, Floater,
Floater–Pham-Trong) all require positive weights on the *whole* one-ring, so none of them applies.
Row 10 (orbifold Tutte) is the only Tutte-plus-extra-rows bijectivity theorem and is specific to
orbifold identifications.

**Why it might be true / sketch.** Row `K` of `L` is `sum_{e in C_K ∩ E_hinge} (x_head - x_tail)`;
under F11 (proved in G4) `C_K ∩ E_hinge = {`hinge edges with target in `K}`, so the row equals
`sum_{v in K} (k_v x_v - sum_{u->v} x_u) = sum_{v in K} (Delta_h X)(v)`. Then
`rank(L) <= min(H, rank Delta_h)` and `dim null(L) = N - rank(L) >= max(N - H, dim ker Delta_h)`,
doubled for the two coordinates. `1 in ker Delta_h` always (row sums vanish), recovering the global
translation. In the pure case `A_S = I` and the condition is exactly `Delta_h X = 0`: **every
interior vertex is the barycentre of its hinge in-neighbours**, which for `k_v = 2` says `x_v` is the
*midpoint* of two in-neighbours. (That special case is a restatement of 2025 Def. 4.1 and is **not**
novel; the general split-component-averaged statement is.)

**Kill experiment.** On **500 random graphs with random `sigma`**, assemble `L` two ways (directly
from Algorithm 1's preimages, and as `A_S * Delta_h`) and require `||L_direct - L_factored||_inf = 0`
exactly (integer entries). **Any mismatch kills it** and simultaneously kills F11. Then check
`dim null(L) >= max(N - H, dim ker Delta_h)` numerically. Runtime: minutes.

**If it survives, the demo.** A figure showing `rho` as a vector field on `M` with the split forest
overlaid, making "the defect is balanced on each split component" visible; and the dimension bound
plotted against measured `dim null(L)` on 500 graphs.

**Risk.** It is a restatement, not a new fact — the bound could be loose everywhere. Mitigation: it
is the algebraic engine for G5 and the right language for stating G4/G5, so it earns its place as
machinery even if it is not a headline.

**Effort.** Derivation S (done). Code S.

### G8. Tutte's theorem fails for Eulerian half-star averaging (injectivity)
**Type: theorem (a counterexample) + a sufficient condition.**

**Claim.** There is a 3-connected planar `M` with `E_split = {}` and a strictly convex fixed
boundary whose unique solution of `Delta_h X = 0` is **not** an embedding (some face inverts);
hence "Tutte auxetic embedding" is a misnomer and no unconditional injectivity theorem exists. A
sufficient condition in the style of GGT Thm 3.13 (every reflex extremum of every hole boundary is a
wheel) is the correct target.

**Gap targeted.** 2026 assumption 11 / Sec. 6 limitation 1: "solutions may exhibit extremely short
edges or sharp angles" and self-intersections "may occur at small opening angles"; `notes/
paper_2026.md` assumption 11 states flatly "**`X` contains embeddings that are not valid embeddings
at all**", and open question 12 asks for a characterization of the valid subset.

**Novelty vs field.** field_tutte route C2 calls this "plausible for the pure case" and proposes to
repair Floater's maximum principle by "formalizing 'alternating subset of the star still
surrounds'". I predict the opposite outcome and propose to settle it by construction. Row 14
(Spriggs, parallel-morph counterexamples) is direct evidence in my favour; row 10 (orbifold Tutte)
is evidence that Tutte-plus-rows bijectivity is not a general theorem; row 4's GGT §6 says the
analogous constrained-parameterization question is open.

**Why it might be true / sketch.** `Delta_h X = 0` puts `x_v` at the barycentre of only the `k_v`
hinge **in**-neighbours, which are every *other* neighbour in cyclic order (Remark A.1 step 5).
Floater Thm 4.1 (field_tutte row 2, full text read by Scout-c) requires positive weights on the
**whole** neighbourhood; with half the star zeroed, the discrete maximum principle still gives
uniqueness (weak diagonal dominance, irreducible if the reversed-hinge digraph reaches the boundary)
but **not** local injectivity: a vertex can be pulled outside the convex hull of its full one-ring
because the omitted neighbours exert no pull. A search should find this quickly on graphs with a
high-valence vertex whose in-neighbours cluster on one side.

**Kill experiment.** Generate **2,000 2-colourable planar graphs** (even interior valency; random
quad/hex subdivisions plus random Delaunay refinements), fix the boundary to a regular convex
polygon, solve `Delta_h X = 0` with `core/tutte_auxetic_solve.cpp`, and compute the signed area of
every face. **If no face has negative or zero signed area in all 2,000 solves, the counterexample
claim is dead** — and the *converse* becomes a strong conjecture worth proving, so the experiment is
informative in both directions. Report the first inverted example minimized by vertex count.
Runtime: minutes.

**If it survives, the demo.** The minimal counterexample as a hero figure (uncut `M` with an
inverted face, next to the exploded `M'`); a rate curve of inversion frequency vs mean interior
valency over 2,000 graphs; and the sufficient condition checked on the non-inverting population.

**Risk.** The counterexample exists but is degenerate in an uninteresting way (a zero-length edge
rather than a genuine fold), or inversions are so common that the interesting question flips to
"characterize the valid subset", which is much harder. Either way the negative result closes an open
question the authors state.

**Effort.** Derivation M (the sufficient condition is the hard half). Code S.

### G9. The non-uniform deployment variety, cut out by cycle functionals on `Gamma`
**Type: characterization.**

**Claim.** The full (non-uniform) deployment configuration space is the real algebraic variety
`V = { omega in R^F : sum_{e in gamma} eps_e (R(omega_{f_1(e)}) - R(omega_{f_2(e)})) x_{u_e} = 0 for
every cycle gamma of Gamma }`, of codimension at most `2 mu(Gamma) = 2H`, containing the uniform
line `omega_f = sigma_f theta/2`; the deployment DOF at that point is
`|F| - 1 - rank(D_omega Phi)`, computable exactly from an `2H x |F|` matrix.

**Gap targeted.** 2026 Sec. 6 limitation 2, verbatim: "patterns that can be uniformly deployed might
also be deployed with a different set of hinge angles. **Analyzing the degrees of freedom of the
deployment space, which could be achieved by sensitivity analysis, is an interesting direction to
explore.**"

**Novelty vs field.** field_kirigami seed 2/5 verdicts: "partly open" / "open for rigid
corner-hinged kirigami on arbitrary graphs", with **R22 IsoGami** the direct threat — but IsoGami
counts zero eigenvalues of `J^T J` **numerically**, per design, on isohedral tilings, and its own
limitations section says extending "to graded patterns and non-periodic materials" is future work.
field_rigidity F13/R1 shows Maxwell counting predicts rigidity for every working pattern, so the
generic combinatorial theories (R2, R4, R6, R7) give the wrong answer here; my formulation is
non-generic and exact. R25 (Li–Zhu–Qu rigid-origami mobility) is the closest published pipeline and
is for folds, not corner pins.

**Why it might be true / sketch.** The derivation is (0.3) with `omega_f` free instead of
`sigma_f theta/2`: the translation potential exists iff the increments
`(R(omega_{f_1}) - R(omega_{f_2})) x_{u_e}` sum to zero around each `Gamma`-cycle. This is exact,
not a linearization, and reduces `3|F|` unknowns to `|F|` by eliminating translations — the
reduction that makes the Maxwell count of F13 uninformative. Linearizing at the uniform point gives
a `2H x |F|` Jacobian whose entries are `J R(omega_f) x_u` terms; its corank is the extra mobility.

**Kill experiment.** On **100 graphs** including rotating squares, kagome, and the paper's Fig. 11
pattern, compute `corank(D_omega Phi)` at `omega = sigma theta/2` for 20 values of `theta`, and
compare against the numerical mobility obtained by counting near-zero singular values of the full
pin-joint constraint Jacobian (`3|F|` DOF, `2|E_hinge|` constraints) from `core/rigidity_jacobian.cpp`.
**If the two mobilities disagree by more than the 3 rigid-body modes on any graph, dead.**
Sanity gate: rotating squares must return mobility exactly 1 (plus rigid motions) for all `theta`.
Runtime: under 20 minutes.

**If it survives, the demo.** A table of deployment DOF vs design DOF (`dim null(L)`) for 20 graphs,
showing they are different numbers; a bifurcation figure where the corank jumps at a special
`theta`; a pattern designed to have exactly 2 deployment DOF and its two-parameter motion.

**Risk.** Heavy overlap with the rigidity persona and with IsoGami's numerical mobility. Mitigation:
the contribution here is the *exact* variety and the reduction to `|F|` angle variables on `Gamma`,
not a numerical mobility count. Second risk: the corank is generically `1` for every reasonable
pattern, making it a confirmation rather than a discovery.

**Effort.** Derivation M. Code M.

### G10. Range-maximizing embeddings from the exact `theta_max`
**Type: algorithm.**

**Claim.** Maximizing the exact `theta_max` of G3 over the shape space
`X = X_0 + sum_i phi_i t_i^T` is a smooth max-min program in the `2 dim null(L)` coefficients `t`,
with closed-form objective and gradients, and it beats 2026 Eq. (9)'s `theta = 0` barrier surrogate
by a large margin on graphs where the binding collision is not at `theta = 0`.

**Gap targeted.** 2026 Sec. 4.5: Eq. (9) is solved **at `theta = 0` only**; `notes/paper_2026.md`
Sec. 8.5 states "Optimizing at `theta = 0` only is itself a heuristic: it penalizes the *initial*
velocity direction at split edges, not collisions at the angle where they actually occur. The paper
does not justify why this suffices"; open question 14 asks whether Eq. (9) actually maximizes
`theta_max`. The barrier `B` and the weight `gamma` are never specified anywhere in the paper
(notes Sec. 13.2, open question 17), so the baseline is under-determined and must be reconstructed.

**Novelty vs field.** field_kirigami seed 1 is "open" with the base paper naming it verbatim.
R18 Liu et al. 2024 maximizes range for enumerated quad pair types only. R22 IsoGami samples tile
shape parameters on a coarse grid and filters by expansion threshold (isogami.txt Sec. 4.2 —
"largely heuristic and could benefit from further automation"), which is exactly the search this
replaces with a gradient method on a closed-form objective.

**Why it might be true / sketch.** By (0.8) each pair contributes `P(t) + Q(t) cos theta +
R(t) sin theta` with `P, Q, R` quadratic in `t`; its smallest positive root is
`theta_pair(t) = atan2(R,Q) - arccos(-P / sqrt(Q^2+R^2))` (branch chosen by screening), smooth
wherever `|P| < sqrt(Q^2+R^2)` and the argmin pair is unique. `theta_max(t) = min_pairs
theta_pair(t)`: a max-min, handled by a smooth log-sum-exp lower bound or by SLP with active-set
updates. Not concave, so only local optima are claimed — the honest claim is a large margin over the
surrogate, not global optimality.

**Kill experiment.** On **25 graphs** with `dim null(L) >= 2` (so there is something to optimize),
compute `theta_max` for: (i) `X_0` from Eq. (6); (ii) the Eq. (9) surrogate with `B` a standard IPC
log barrier and `gamma` swept over `{1e-3, 1e-2, 1e-1, 1}` (best result kept, to be fair to the
baseline); (iii) this method. **If the median relative gain of (iii) over the best (ii) is below
15%, dead.** Runtime: under 30 minutes.

**If it survives, the demo.** `theta_max` bar chart across 25 graphs for the three methods; the hero
example is a pattern the surrogate opens to a small angle and this method opens to near its
kinematic limit, shown as a deployment strip; fabricable SVG export at both `theta = 0` and
`theta_max`.

**Risk.** Eq. (9)'s surrogate may already be near-optimal because the first collinearity really does
happen near `theta = 0` in practice, in which case the gain is small and the contribution collapses
to "we made the objective exact". Depends entirely on G3.

**Effort.** Derivation S. Code L.

## 2. Self-attack: hostile SIGGRAPH/TOG reviewer on my top 3

### Against G2 (conformality theorem) — **Reject.**
This is a two-line observation dressed as a theorem. Once you write `P_theta = cos(theta/2) P_0 +
sin(theta/2) Q` — which follows immediately from the fact that rigid faces rotate by half the
opening angle, a fact every practitioner in this area has known since Grima and Evans wrote down
rotating squares in 2000 — the "theorem" is that a linear pencil `cI + sW` is conformal iff `W` is.
That is undergraduate linear algebra. Segall et al. did not prove it because it did not need
proving; they wrote "we observe" because they were being careful about a trivial point, not because
they were stuck. The submission's own novelty argument is that IsoGami used numerical continuation,
but IsoGami solves a *different and harder* problem (non-uniform, contact-aware trajectories), so
their choice of method says nothing about whether this identity is known. A theorem whose entire
content is that a stated empirical observation is in fact trivially true is a paragraph in a revised
version of the original paper, not a paper.

### Against G3/G10 (exact collision times and range maximization) — **Reject.**
The reduction to `P + Q cos theta + R sin theta = 0` handles *collinearity of two straight edges*.
Actual collision-freeness of a planar assembly of polygons is a containment problem over
`O(|E'|^2)` pairs with a combinatorial overlap predicate that the submission relegates to "an
overlap screen at the candidate roots" and never analyses. That screen is where all the difficulty
lives, and the paper does not tell me whether it is `O(1)` or a full arrangement computation. Worse,
the whole edifice presumes the uniform-`theta` path; the moment a face is not perfectly rigid, or a
hinge has thickness, or the cut is curved (2026 Sec. 4.6, which the submission ignores entirely),
the closed form evaporates and you are back to simulation. Finally the headline experiment compares
against a baseline the submission had to *invent*, because Segall et al. never specify `B` or
`gamma`. Beating a baseline you reconstructed yourself, with a barrier of your choosing, at a
`gamma` you swept, is not a comparison. On the theory side, "collisions arise immediately after
collinearity" is quoted from the very paper being improved and is itself unproved there.

### Against G6 (T-join / exact `sigma`) — **Reject.**
Two separate problems. First, the "NP-hardness is wrong" claim is a misreading dressed as a
correction: Segall et al. say max-cut is NP-hard, which is true in general, and they use a fast
heuristic. That max-cut is polynomial on *planar* graphs is Hadlock 1975, taught in every
combinatorial optimization course, and the observation that a dual max-cut is a primal T-join is the
standard textbook proof of Hadlock's theorem. There is no new mathematics here at all — the only new
content is the sentence "the dual graph in this application happens to be planar", which any referee
would expect the authors to fix in a camera-ready. Second, the submission itself concedes that
`|E_split|` is a *proxy*: the paper's stated goal is "many balanced, small holes", which is neither
defined nor equal to minimum split count, and the submission's own G6 risk paragraph admits that
minimizing `|E_split|` shrinks the design space that G4/G5/G7 are all about maximizing. So the
"exact" algorithm optimizes an objective the submission argues elsewhere is the wrong one. Solving
the wrong problem optimally is worse than solving it approximately.

**My response to my own attacks, briefly and honestly.**
- G2 survives only bundled with G1 and G3 as one contribution: *a complete closed-form theory of
  uniform deployment on arbitrary planar graphs*, of which conformality is one corollary and exact
  `theta_max` another. Alone it is a paragraph. The reviewer is right about that.
- G3's overlap screen is a genuine unresolved gap in my sketch and must be settled **before**
  commitment. The baseline-reconstruction objection is real and the only defence is to publish the
  reconstructed baseline and sweep `gamma` honestly, as G10's kill experiment already does.
- G6's algorithmic half is genuinely weak (Hadlock 1975 is the right citation and I should lead with
  it, not against it). Its **theorem** half — the parity identity and the resulting lower bound
  `dim(shape space) >= |T| + 2 dim Z`, i.e. *odd-valency vertices force design freedom* — is the
  part that is new and that no field row covers. Reframe G6 around the bound, demote the algorithm.

  cost.
